Single-sided magnitude, corrected for window coherent gain. Off-bin peaks may be smaller than true tone amplitude.

03 / Phase spectrum

Phase of the windowed DFT in degrees; bins below 1% of the largest magnitude for each trace are hidden. Phase is referenced to the record start.

04 / Spectrogram (STFT)

Hann or rectangular window, 75% overlap, relative to maximum magnitude in the displayed record. Dark = −60 dB; bright = 0 dB. Frame centres are shown; no data outside complete frames.

06 / Butterworth filter response

Group delay is the negative derivative of phase with respect to angular frequency; values near spectral nulls are omitted. It is not wall-clock processing latency.

Frequency axis is logarithmic. Low/high-pass cutoffs are exact −3.01 dB points of each edge; band-pass is a cascade, not a canonical band-pass design. Phase is unwrapped on the sampled grid.

07 / Pole-zero map (z-plane)

08 / Inverse FFT & spectral reconstruction

Raw, unwindowed DFT; conjugate pairs are preserved for a real result. The selection is ranked by energy, not frequency.

09 / Linear convolution

Full linear convolution with zero extension; the output extends M−1 samples past the input. The moving-average filter is causal.

Sampling & FFT

x[n] = x(n / fs)
X[k] = Σ x[n] e^(−j2πkn/N)
Δf = fs / N

The FFT evaluates the DFT in O(N log N). Above fs/2, sampled frequencies fold into the Nyquist interval.

Filtering & Fourier series

H(z) = B(z) / A(z)
y[n] = Σ b[k]x[n−k] − Σ a[k]y[n−k]

Butterworth filters use prewarped bilinear transformation and cascaded first/second-order sections. Odd sine harmonics approximate a square wave and exhibit Gibbs overshoot.
